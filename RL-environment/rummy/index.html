New game of Rummy

Rummy

Seven cards. A discard pile you can dig into.

Every card you put on the table scores for you. Every card still in your hand at the end counts against you.

  1. Draw the top of the stock, or any discard together with every card above it. A card from the pile can’t go straight back.
  2. A card dug out from under the top must go into a meld or lay-off that same turn.
  3. Lay three of a rank, or three in a row of a suit (runs wrap: Q–K–A). Longer melds go down as three plus lay-offs; select them all to lay them at once.
  4. Add cards to anyone’s melds. Each card scores its points for you.
  5. Discard to end your turn. Discard your last card to end the round.

    How the agent was trained

    How the agent was trained

    The AI you play is a small transformer, 8.9M parameters, that learned Rummy from scratch by playing against itself: about 62 hours on one Mac GPU and roughly 1.8 billion decisions over 29 million games. It only sees what you see: its own hand, the table, the discard pile and what each opponent did.

    01 · Encoding

    One token per turn, and each one restates the table.

    STOCK5♣6♣7♣DISC K♦your decisions TAKE 9♥ · DISC 2♠opponent 1 STOCK · +8♣ · DISC Q♣opponent 2 ?next

    In Plump a hand only shrinks. In Rummy cards keep arriving, so instead of making the model rebuild the hand from the history, every decision token states the present: for each of the 52 cards, where its copies are (unseen, your hand, an opponent who took it from the pile, the table and who laid it, or the discard pile by depth) and whether it can be laid off right now. Each opponent turn is one token with what it drew, dug out, melded and threw. Those are the tells.

    A small card grid reads the 52 cards first. It convolves around the 13-rank cycle, since runs wrap from King to Ace, and pools each rank over suits for sets. Its output becomes part of the token, and a 12-layer causal transformer reads the stream, about 40 tokens a game, with a key-value cache in self-play.

    02 · Actions & objective

    Score every legal move. Beat the table average.

    The rules engine lists every legal move: the stock, the top discard, any deeper card you could use, every three-card meld, lay-off and discard. Each move is embedded from its cards (for a lay-off also the meld it joins, for a pickup every card it takes) and scored against the transformer’s state. Melds go down three cards at a time, so every further card is its own choice: table one more, or stop.

    The reward is your round score minus the average opponent score. Every seat is the same network, trained with PPO. An oracle critic that sees every hand and the next stock card sets the baseline during training only. Side heads predict which cards each opponent holds, who goes out, and what each seat is left holding.

    Table log

      Rules

      How to play Rummy

      Seven cards each, one or two decks, no jokers. Every turn has three parts:

      1. Draw. Take the top card of the stock, or any card in the discard pile together with every card above it. The top card alone you may simply keep, but not discard again the same turn.
      2. Meld. A new meld always goes down as three cards: a set is three of the same rank in different suits, a run three of one suit in order. Runs wrap, so Q–K–A and K–A–2 both work. Every further card is a lay-off, added one at a time to any meld on the table, anyone’s: a set grows to four, a run up to all 13. Lay as many as you like.
      3. Discard one card to end your turn. A meld or lay-off can never use your last card: you go out by discarding it.

      Digging into the pile

      Taking a card from below the top brings every card above it into your hand too, and the card you dug out must go into a meld or lay-off that same turn. You can only pick a deeper card you can use right away: in a meld, as a lay-off, or at the end of a run on the table you extend up to it from your hand.

      Scoring

      Every card you meld or lay off scores its points for you: number cards their value, J, Q and K 10, an Ace 11 (1 when it starts a run like A–2–3). When the round ends, the cards left in each hand count against their owner.

      The round ends when someone discards their last card, or after the turn that draws the last stock card. A 200-turn limit ends endless discard loops.
