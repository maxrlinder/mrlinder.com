Hjärter

Swedish Hearts. Three to five players.

Every heart you take costs a point and the queen of spades thirteen. Dodge them all deal after deal, or take every one and send the points to everyone else.

  1. Pass two cards before each deal: to the right, to the left, across (four players), then keep them. The cycle repeats.
  2. The 2♣ leads the first trick (the 3♣ with five players). No hearts and no Q♠ in the first trick.
  3. Follow suit if you can; otherwise play anything. The highest card of the suit led takes the trick. No trumps.
  4. Hearts may lead a trick only once a heart has been discarded.
  5. Take the moon, all thirteen hearts and Q♠, and everyone else scores 26. The game ends when someone reaches 100; the lowest score wins.

    How the agent was trained

    How the agent was trained

    The AI you play is a small transformer, 8.7M parameters, that learned Hjärter from scratch by playing against itself: 50 million single deals, then 2.0 million whole games to 100, about 61 hours on one desktop GPU. It only sees what you see: its own hand, the cards it passed and was passed, every card played and the score.

    01 · Architecture

    Every card is a token. Two heads score the 52 cards.

    Each token is one card: dealt to the agent, passed by it, passed to it, or played by anyone. It also says who acts next, the trick so far, the points every seat has taken this deal, and the table's rules: how many cards are passed, and whether the jack of diamonds counts −10. In whole games every token carries the score too: the agent's lead over each player, its place, and how far it and the leader are from 100.

    A 12-layer causal transformer reads the stream. Two heads score the 52 cards, one for the pass (a card at a time) and one for play. A card's score comes from its rank, its suit and the card itself, so the queen of spades can be unlike other spades and other queens. Illegal cards are masked, and the softmax over the rest is the policy. A move with a single legal card is played for it.

    02 · Objective

    First the deal, then the game.

    Every seat is the same network, trained with PPO. In single deals the reward was the deal's points against the table's mean. The fine-tune plays whole games to 100 and is rewarded only for the final place, from +1 for the lowest score to −1 for the highest. It sees the score, so it can play a deal differently when it leads, trails, or the game is about to end.

    An oracle critic that sees every hand sets the baseline during training only. The agent's own heads estimate two values: the final place, which it is trained for, and your points this deal against the table, which the Advantage switch shows. Side heads predict who holds each unseen card, every seat's points at the end of the deal and who wins the trick.

      Rules

      How to play Hjärter

      Hjärter is the Swedish name for Hearts. These are a regular table’s house rules; the setup screen lets you change the passing, the jack of diamonds and the length of the game.

      The deal

      Three to five players, 52 cards, ace high, no trumps. Cards are removed so everyone gets the same number: with three players the 2♦ (17 cards each), with five the 2♦ and 2♣ (10 each); four players get 13 each. The deal moves to the left every hand.

      Passing

      Before play, everyone passes two cards to the same neighbour, face down: to the right in the first deal, to the left in the second, across in the third (four players only), and nobody passes in the next. Then the cycle starts over. You see the cards you get only once everyone has passed.

      Play

      • Whoever holds the 2♣ (the 3♣ with five players) leads it to the first trick.
      • Follow the suit led if you can. If you cannot, play any card. The highest card of the suit led takes the trick, and its winner leads the next.
      • First trick: no hearts and no Q♠, unless your hand holds nothing else.
      • Hearts: you may lead a heart only once hearts are broken, when a heart has been played to a trick, or when you hold nothing but hearts.

      Scoring

      Each heart you take scores 1 and the queen of spades 13: 26 points a deal, and points are bad. Take all thirteen hearts and the Q♠, the moon, and you score 0 while everyone else scores 26. With the jack of diamonds option, whoever takes the J♦ scores −10; it is not a penalty card and does not count towards the moon.

      After the deal in which someone reaches the end score (100 unless you chose otherwise), the game is over and the lowest score wins.

      You play every card yourself, even your only legal one, like leading the 2♣, unless auto-play has your hand.
